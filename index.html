تَكَلُّم

Speaking drills in standard Arabic. Say every sentence out loud.

About Takallum

Takallum (تَكَلُّم, “speaking”) is a free speaking-practice app for Modern Standard Arabic (fusha). Instead of reading about grammar, you say whole sentences out loud, compare them with a fully vowelled model answer and its audio, and mark yourself honestly. Sentences you miss come back sooner.

Three ways to practise

  • Swap-in drills. 18 sentence patterns and 136 sentences, from everyday phrases like أُرِيدُ أَنْ (I want to) and أَيْنَ (where is) to grammar builders: لَمْ (didn’t), كَانَ and its sisters, إِنَّ and its sisters, مُنْذُ, يَجِبُ, الَّذِي, the idafa, comparatives, conditionals with إِذَا and لَوْ, and numbers.
  • Transform chains. 7 chains where one sentence changes step by step: a new person, tense, gender or negative each time.
  • Role-play. 6 everyday conversations: meeting someone, at the shop, asking the way, visiting a home, at the doctor’s and talking about yesterday.

Questions

Is Takallum free?

Yes. It is free and needs no sign-up. Your progress is saved in your browser on this device.

Which kind of Arabic does it teach?

Modern Standard Arabic (fusha), the formal Arabic used in books, news and education and understood across the Arab world. Every sentence is fully vowelled, and you can switch the vowel marks off in settings once you can read without them.

Who is it for?

Beginner to intermediate learners who can read Arabic script and want to turn the grammar they have studied into sentences they can actually say.

Does it have audio?

Yes. Every Arabic sentence plays at normal or slow speed using your device’s Arabic text-to-speech voice. Without an Arabic voice the drills still work, just without sound.

How does it decide what to practise?

It uses spaced repetition. A sentence you get right moves up a level; one you miss drops back and comes round again later in the same session. Mixed review gathers your ten weakest sentences from every pattern.
